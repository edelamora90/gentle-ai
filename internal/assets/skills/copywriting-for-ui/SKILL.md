# Copywriting for UI

Copy is design material, not decoration. Before writing anything, ask what the interface needs to say to be easier to understand and use.

## Principles

- **Write from the user's side.** Name things by what the person controls or recognizes, never by how the system is built. Someone "manages notifications", they do not "configure webhooks".
- **Active voice by default.** A control says exactly what happens when used: "Save changes", not "Submit". An action's name stays identical across the whole flow — if the button says "Publish", the confirmation says "Published".
- **Specific beats clever.** "Free for your first 5 projects" is worth more than "special limited-time offer".
- **Empty states and errors are moments of direction, not of human tone.** Explain what happened and how to fix it, in the interface's voice, without apologizing and without being vague.
- **Conversational but calibrated register**: simple verbs, no filler, tone matched to the brand and audience. Each element does exactly one job — a label labels, an example exemplifies.

## Checklist before delivering copy

- [ ] Does every CTA describe the action rather than a generic "Submit"/"Get started"?
- [ ] Are numbers and claims specific (with a figure, deadline, or real condition) instead of vague ("fast", "easy", "amazing")?
- [ ] Does the copy avoid internal system or company jargon the user does not recognize?
- [ ] Is the same verb/action name repeated across the whole related flow?
- [ ] If the client already has real content (a document, current site, brief with final copy): was it used as-is, without "improving" it uninvited?

## When NOT to rewrite

If the user already brings the final copy (their own approved content), do not reinterpret or "optimize" it on your own initiative — your job there is to lay it out well, not to rewrite it. Flag suggestions as suggestions; never apply them directly.
